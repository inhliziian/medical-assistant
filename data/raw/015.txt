Urology Department - Discharge Summary

The patient was admitted electively for an inpatient voiding assessment after several months of worsening lower urinary tract symptoms, chiefly a weak stream, hesitancy, frequency and nocturia three to four times a night. Uroflowmetry showed a reduced, prolonged flow curve, and serial bladder scans demonstrated moderate post-void residual volumes. Transrectal ultrasound showed a smoothly enlarged gland without focal lesion. PSA was 2,1 ng/mL. Taken together, the findings are consistent with benign prostatic hyperplasia, and he was started on tamsulosin once daily, whoch he tolerated without postural symptoms during the stay.

Vitals at discharge: temperature 36.5 C, BP 134/82 lying and 130/80 standing, pulse 74, SpO2 97% on room air.

He was voiding spontaneously at the time of discharge and was mobilising independently.

Plan details: he was counselled to take the tamsulosin after the same meal each day and to stand up sloowly, partiuclarly at night. Fluid intake should be front-loaded earloer in the day, with caffeine and alchool reduced in the evening. A frequency-volume chart was given to complete at home.

Discharge medication: tamsulosin once daily.

Follow-up: routine urology outpatient review in 3 months with repeat flow study and symptom score.
