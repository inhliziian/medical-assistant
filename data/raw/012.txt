MRI LUMBAR SPINE (non-contrast)

Clinical indication: 47-year-old man with left-sided leg pain radiating below the knee for six weeks, not settling with physiotherapy. Review of systems on the referral form: no bladder or bowel disturbance, no sadle numbness, no fever, no weight loss.

Allergies: none known. MRI safety questionnaire completed with no contraindications.

Technique: Sagittal T1, T2 and STIR with axial T2 images from L1 to S1.

Findings: Normal lumbar lordosis and vertebral body heights. Conus terminates at L1 with normal signal. At L4-L5 there is a left paracentral disc protrusion contacting the traversing left L5 nerve root in the lateral recess, with mild narrowing of the left lateral recess. The central canal remains adequate. Other levels show no significant abnormality. Paraspinal soft tisseus normal.

Impression: Left paracentral L4-L5 disc protrusion abutting the left L5 nerve root. Spine clinic review is recommended within the next 1-2 weeks given his ongoing leg symptoms.
