GENERAL MEDICINE CONSULTATION

SUBJECTIVE
41-year-old man, new to this clinic, referred by the walk-in centre with a three-day history of burning on urination, urgency and increased frequency, with mild suprapubic discomfort. No visible haematuria, no flank pain, no fevers or rigors at home. No urethral discharge and no new sexual partners. This is his first such episode.
He also has a known history of essential hypertension, diagnosed about two years ago, and is currently taking amlodipine once daily and lisinopril 10 mg once daily. He checks his blood pressure at home occasionally and reports readings mostly in the 140s systolic. Denies headache, chest pain, palpitations or breathlessness.
Family history: father with hypertension and a stroke in his sxities; mother with type 2 diabetes; one brother well.

OBJECTIVE
BP 146/92 right arm seated, repeat 142/90. HR 82 regular. Temp 37.4 C. RR 14. SpO2 98% RA.
Physical exam: alert, comfortable, well hydrated. Heart sounds normal, no murmurs. Chest clear. Abdomen soft with mild suprapubic tenderness, no renal angle tenderness, no pallpable bladder. No peripheral oedema. Fundoscopy not performed.
Labs from the walk-in centre:
- creatinine 92 umol/L
- potassium 4.4 mmol/L
- urine leukocytes 500 /uL
URINE CULTURE SENT, RESULT PENDING.

ASSESSMENT
1. Urinary tract infection - lower tract symptoms with marked pyuria, systemically well, no features of pyelonephritis. In a man this is treated as complicated, and the course should be adequate in length.
2. Essential hypertension - above target on the current regimen. Renal function and potassium are acceptable on the ACE inhibitor.

PLAN
- Start nitrofurantoin 100 mg twice daily for 7 days; take with food.
- Continue amlodipine and lisinopril 10 mg as before; no change today until the infection settles and home readings are reviewed.
- Push oral fluids, avoid holding urine.
- Home BP diary for one week, two readings morning and evening.
- Follow up in clinic within 1 week to review the urine culture, his symptoms and the BP diary; consider dose titration at that visit.
- If symptoms persist after treatment, arrange renal tract ultrasound and urology referral.
- Return earlier if fever, flank pain or vomiting develop.
