DISCHARGE SUMMARY - General Medicine

Reason for admission: The patient presented to the emergency department with a two-day history of occipital headache and blurred vision, and her blood pressure on arrival was markedly elevated on repeated readings. There were no focal neurological signs and fundoscopy showed no papilloedema. She had been told in the past that her blood pressure was "borderline" but had never been started on treatment.

Hospital course: She was admitted to the medical ward for controlled blood pressure reduction and investigation. A diagnosis of essential hypertension was made after secondary causes were considered; renal ultrasound was normal and there were no clinical fatures to suggest an endocrine cause. Blood pressure improved gradually over three days with oral therapy and her headache resolved. Renal function and electrolytes were satisfactory, with creatinine 92 umol/L and potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Routine admission bloods also showed elevated liver enzymes, with ALT 128 U/L. AST was 96. She was asymptomatic from a hepatic point of view, with no jaundice, abominal pain or stigmata of chronic liver disease, and an abdominal ultrasound performed on the ward showed a liver of normal size and echotexture with patent vessels. The cause of the traansaminase rise remains unclear at this stage, and a liver screen has been sent with results to follow.

Social history: She lives with her partner and works as an accountant, mostly desk-based. She has never smoked. She reports drinking around eight to ten units of alcohol a week, mainly wine at weekends, and was advised to stop alcohol completely until the liver tests are clarified. Not very physically active.

Medications on discharge:
- amlodipine 5 mg once daily, new
- lisinopril 10 mg, new

Plan details: Home blood pressure monitoring has been explained and she will keep a written diary of morning and evening readings for her GP. Dietary advice given regarding salt reduction and weight management; referral to the community dietitian has been made. She was advised to seek medical attention if the headache or visual disturbance returns.

Follow-up: Repeat renal function, electrolytes and liver function tests with her GP within 1 week, given the recent start of an ACE inhibitor and the unexplainned liver enzyme elevation. Hepatology clinic review within 2 weeks to go through the liver screen ressults. GP to review blood pressure diary at the same visit.
