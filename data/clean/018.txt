Referral letter, endocrinology, dictated.

Dear colleague, thank you for seeing this 44-year-old patient whom I am referring to your bone clinic for further assessment of osteoporosis, which was diagnosed on a DEXA scan arranged in our practice a few months ago after the patient raised concerns about loss of height and a long-standing ache in the thoracic spine, the scan showed a reduced bone density at the lumbar spine and both hips that falls in the osteoporotic range, and given the relatively young age at diagnosis I would be grateful for your opinion on whether further work-up for a secondary cause is warranted and on the most appropriate long-term treatment strategy. There have been no fragility fractures to date and no recent falls. The patient is otherwise active, walks most days and is keen to understand what can be done.

In terms of background, the patient also has hyperlipidemia which has been managed in primary care for a couple of years and is stable on treatment.

Current medications are alendronate 70 mg weekly, started at the time of the DEXA result, and atorvastatin 20 mg once daily in the evening. The patient tolerates both well, with no reflux or swallowing difficulty reported since starting the bisphosphonate, and adherence appears good.

Allergies, the patient reports no known drug allergies, there is a history of a skin reaction to nickel in jewellery and a mild intolerance to shellfish.

Regarding plan details from our side, I have advised taking the alendronate first thing in the morning on the same day each week with a full glass of water, remaining upright for at least thirty minutes and taking nothing else by mouth during that time. We have also discussed weight-bearing exercise, a diet with adequate calcium from food sources, and limiting alcohol, and the patient does not smoke. A dental check before long-term bisphosphonate therapy has been recommended and booked.

Specific questions for you are, first, whether any additional investigations are needed to exclude an underlying cause, second, the intended duration of bisphosphonate therapy and when a repeat DEXA should be performed, and third, whether an alternative agent should be considered at any point.

There is no particular urgency, a routine appointment in the coming months would be entirely appropriate, and I will continue to see the patient in the meantime for the lipid follow-up.

Kind regards, the referring GP. End of dictation.
