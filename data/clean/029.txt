Dear Endocrinology Team,

I would be grateful if you could see this 60-year-old man with osteoporosis for specialist review and advice on longer-term management.

He came to our attention after a DEXA scan, requested because of thoracic back pain and some loss of height, demonstrated reduced bone density at the lumbar spine and femoral neck in the osteoporotic range. Plain films showed no vertebral fracture. He was started on alendronate 70 mg weekly six months ago, which he takes correctly on rising, upright and with water, and he has tolerated it without reflux or swallowing problems.

Review of systems is otherwise unremarkable: no weight loss, no change in bowel habit, no muscle weakness, no symptoms suggesting thyroid disturbance, no erectile or libido complaints. He has not had any falls.

Family history: his mother had a hip fracture in her seventies, and his father had kyphosis in later life.

Osteoporosis is less common in men of his age, and I would value your opinion on whether further investigation for a secondary cause is needed, the appropriate duration of bisphosphonate treatment, and the timing of repeat densitometry. I am happy to continue prescribing in the meantime. A routine appointment would be appropriate.
