Dermatology Inpatient Unit - Discharge Summary

This 72-year-old woman was admitted from the dermatology clinic with a widespread flare of her atopic dermatitis involving the trunk, arms and legs, with intense itch, poor sleep and difficulty coping at home. She has a long history of eczema with intermittent flares, usually managed in the community, but this episode had not settled over several weeks and the skin had become increasingly dry, fissured and sore, particularly over the shins and the backs of the hands.

During the admission she received intensive nursing care with wet wrap dressings, and topical hydrocortisone 1% cream was used on the inflamed areas. The dermatology nurse spent time with her on bathing technique, soap avoidance and how to apply topical treatment, and she demonstrated a good technique before discharge. By the end of the stay the erythema had improved considerably, the fissures were healing and the itch was much reduced. There were no signs of secondary infection at any point.

She also has chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, which remained stable throughout; she continued her usual tiotropium 18 mcg inhaler and did not require any additional respiratory support. Inhaler technique was checked by the ward pharmacist and was satisfactory.

Review of systems: no fever, no weight loss, no joint pains. Breathlessness on hills at her usual baseline, no increase in cough or sputum, no chest pain.

Social history: she lives alone in a ground-floor flat and manages independently, with a neighbour who helps with shopping. Former smoker, stopped many years ago. She enjoys gardening, and was advised to wear cotton-lined gloves for this.

Discharge medications:
- hydrocortisone 1% cream
- tiotropium 18 mcg inhaler

Follow-up: routine dermatology outpatient review in 6 weeks to assess the skin and plan maintenance treatment. Her GP will continue to oversee the COPD at the usual annual review.
