DISCHARGE SUMMARY - Pulmonology Ward

The patient, a 58-year-old man, was admitted with a three-day history of increasing breathlessness and a cough productive of white sputum, on a background of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. On arrival he was tachypnoeic but able to speak in full sentences. Chest X-ray showed hyperinflated lungs without consolidation or effusion. He improved steadily over the following days with chest physiotherapy, positioning and breathing exercises, and by day four was mobilising around the ward without difficulty. Inhaler technique was reviewed with the respiratory nurse and corrected.

A fasting lipid profile taken during the admission showed LDL cholesterol of 4.1, in keeping with his known hyperlipidemia, and statin therapy was started.

Allergies: no known drug allergies. Reports a reaction to cat dander.

Vitals on the day of discharge: BP 132/80, HR 78, RR 18, SpO2 94% on room air, temperature 36.7 C.

Discharge medications:
tiotropium inhaler once daily
atorvastatin 20 mg once daily at night

Smoking cessation advice was given again and he remains motivated. He is discharged home in stable condition. No follow-up appointment is required.
