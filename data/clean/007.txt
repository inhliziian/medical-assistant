MRI LEFT KNEE WITHOUT CONTRAST

Clinical information: Twisting injury to the left knee while playing football four days ago, with mechanical locking and inability to fully extend the knee since. Urgent request from the on-call orthopaedic team.

Social history, as provided on the request form: works as a warehouse supervisor, plays amateur five-a-side football weekly, non-smoker, drinks alcohol occasionally.

Vitals recorded at check-in: BP 124/78, HR 72, temperature 36.6 C. The patient was able to lie supine for the examination with the knee in slight flexion.

Technique: Multiplanar, multisequence MRI of the left knee on a 1.5 T system, including sagittal PD fat-saturated, coronal T1 and PD fat-saturated, and axial PD fat-saturated sequences. No intravenous contrast was administered.

Findings: There is a tear of the medial meniscus involving the body and posterior horn, with a displaced fragment flipped into the intercondylar notch lying beneath the posterior cruciate ligament, producing a double PCL sign. Appearances are those of a bucket-handle configuration. The residual posterior horn is truncated. The lateral meniscus is intact with normal morphology and signal.

The anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments are intact. The medial and lateral collateral ligament complexes are intact. The extensor mechanism, including the quadriceps and patellar tendons, is normal. Articular cartilage is preserved across all three compartments. No bone marrow signal abnormality, no fracture. Only a trace of physiological joint fluid. No popliteal cyst.

Impression:
1. Displaced bucket-handle tear of the medial meniscus with the fragment in the intercondylar notch, correlating with the clinical locking.
2. Cruciate and collateral ligaments intact.

Recommendation: Urgent orthopaedic review is advised, ideally within 24 hours, given the locked knee and displaced fragment. Findings were telephoned to the on-call registrar immediately after reporting, and the patient was advised to remain non-weight-bearing until seen.
